**Task B: Product Analysis**

The primary function of a rubber mallet is to transfer impact force to an object while minimizing surface damage. The handle lets the user generate and control motion, while the rubber head transfers force during impact and deforms to reduce the severity of the contact. 

The primary behavior can be modeled using kinetic energy: KE = 1/2mv^2, where KE is kinetic energy, (m) is the mass of the mallet, and (v) is its velocity before impact. The mallet uses this kinetic energy to transfer force to the object being struck.
Assumption: The mallet is treated as a rigid body immediately before impact, meaning its deformation is small enough that its kinetic energy can be approximated using this equation.

The rubber head has a large, flat striking surface that spreads the impact force over a larger area. Its rounded edges and flexible material help reduce the chance of damaging the object being struck.

The long handle gives the user leverage and allows the head to reach a higher velocity during the swing. Its shape provides a comfortable gripping area while remaining strong enough to transfer the user’s force to the head.

A relevant patent is U.S. Patent No. 10,682,746 B2, originally published as US20180036872A1, titled Rubber Mallet. The inventor is Cheng-Wei Su. The patent describes a rubber mallet with a handle and a multi-material striking head designed to provide strength and effective energy transfer while reducing manufacturing costs.

One design decision in the patent is using two different materials in the striking head. I think this was done to balance durability and impact performance. The outer material provides a softer contact surface, while the inner material provides additional strength and helps transfer energy from the handle to the object.

Overall, the rubber mallet is a simple mechanical product, but its design demonstrates important engineering concepts such as energy transfer, material selection, force distribution, and deformation. 

Alternative Solutions:

Plastic Mallet: uses a polymer head to provide a controlled impact with less surface damage
Leather Mallet: uses a softer leather striking surface for applications where protecting the contacted surface is important.
